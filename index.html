Questions clients ask

The practical details before we start.

Can you start from a rough idea, sketch or incomplete CAD?

Yes. Work can begin from an early concept, sketches, reference dimensions or an existing CAD model, depending on the stage of the project.

Can you handle both the design and the simulation?

Yes. Product design, CFD and FEA can be kept in one workflow so simulation findings can feed directly back into the geometry.

What do you need from me to quote the work?

A short description of the problem, any available drawings or CAD, target performance, known loads or flow conditions, required deliverables and your preferred timeline are usually enough for an initial scope.

Can the work be quoted as a fixed-price project?

For clearly defined deliverables, fixed-price work is possible. Larger or evolving projects can be divided into milestones so scope and cost stay visible as the design develops.

What files and deliverables can I receive?

Depending on scope, deliverables can include native CAD, STEP files, manufacturing drawings, assemblies, BOMs, simulation reports, plots and the supporting engineering documentation agreed for the project.

Can you work under an NDA or with confidential product data?

If the project is confidential, mention that before sending sensitive files. NDA and information-handling requirements can be agreed before detailed project data is shared.

Which CAD and simulation tools do you use?

The toolset includes SolidWorks, Catia V5, Fusion 360 and AutoCAD for design, plus Ansys, Abaqus and SolidWorks Simulation for structural, thermal and fluid analysis.

Can you work with my existing engineer, manufacturer or supplier?

Yes. The work can be structured around an existing team, with design files, drawings and engineering decisions prepared for review or handoff to manufacturing.

Do you work with international clients remotely?

Yes. The engineering workflow is suitable for remote projects, with requirements, CAD, reviews and deliverables handled digitally.

How are revisions and scope changes handled?

The initial scope should define the expected deliverables and review points. If the requirements change materially, the impact on schedule and cost can be agreed before the additional work proceeds.

Can you help if a design is failing or underperforming?

Yes. Existing designs can be reviewed to identify likely structural, thermal, flow or manufacturability issues, then tested through targeted analysis and design changes.

Are CFD or FEA results the same as physical certification?

No. Simulation is an engineering decision tool and depends on the model, assumptions, inputs and boundary conditions. Where certification or regulatory approval is required, the relevant physical tests and authorised certification process still apply.
